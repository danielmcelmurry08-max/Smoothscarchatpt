Published clinical research

What was actually tested?

A 2024 randomized single-blind split-scar study included 32 participants with postoperative wounds that had united. One half of each scar received a SPARC-targeting siRNA dissolving microneedle patch, and the other half received a silicone sheet.

The mean reduction in scar volume was 83.8% on the studied siRNA-treated side and 74.1% on the silicone-treated side: a 9.7-percentage-point difference. These are group averages from the specific tested intervention, not results promised for each individual scar.

The study used daily application for 12 hours. Its tested siRNA formulation and schedule cannot automatically be equated with the hydrolyzed-RNA products or 8-hour and twice-weekly routines sold here. It does not establish BioRNA-C-specific or C-section-specific clinical outcomes.

Source: 2024 British Journal of Dermatology randomized trial. Start product use only after the wound is fully closed and follow supplied instructions.

How it works

More than surface coverage

A raised scar forms when the skin makes too much repair tissue while it heals. BioRNA uses dissolving micro-tips made of hyaluronic acid with hydrolyzed RNA. Hyaluronic acid draws in moisture, while the patch protects the scar and helps keep the ingredients against the skin during overnight wear.

  1. Nanoplex

    The research technology packs RNA into a tiny bundle. This describes the experimental siRNA platform; it is not proof that every retail hydrolyzed-RNA patch has the same formulation or effect.

  2. Dissolvable micro-tips

    Hyaluronic acid micro-tips dissolve after application.

  3. Ingredient transfer

    The tips release their ingredients into the upper skin layers. Follow the supplied product instructions.

  4. Reduced scar appearance

    Scar appearance may change over time. A flatter or softer result is not guaranteed.
