Scar-care evidence, explained simply.

Understand what the BioRNA findings say, and what they cannot promise.

Two studies, two numbers.

50 people · 60 days · up to 95% scar-volume reduction in a small manufacturer-reported study. “Up to” is the highest result, not the average. Individual results vary.

32 participants · 60 days · 83.8% vs 74.1% mean scar-volume reduction in a separate British Journal of Dermatology randomized split-scar trial: studied siRNA side versus silicone-sheet side.

These numbers describe different studies and different summaries, so they are not contradictory or interchangeable. The published trial used a specific SPARC-targeting siRNA formulation and daily 12-hour application. It does not establish identical outcomes for the marketed hydrolyzed-RNA patches, BioRNA-C, or C-section scars.

Published clinical research

What was actually tested?

A 2024 randomized single-blind split-scar study included 32 participants with postoperative wounds that had united. One half of each scar received a SPARC-targeting siRNA dissolving microneedle patch, and the other half received a silicone sheet.

After 60 days, the mean reduction in scar volume was 83.8% on the studied siRNA-treated side and 74.1% on the silicone-treated side: a 9.7-percentage-point difference. These are group averages from the specific tested intervention, not results promised for each individual scar.

The study used daily application for 12 hours. Its tested siRNA formulation and schedule cannot automatically be equated with the hydrolyzed-RNA products or 8-hour and twice-weekly routines sold here. It does not establish BioRNA-C-specific or C-section-specific clinical outcomes.

Source: 2024 British Journal of Dermatology randomized trial. Start product use only after the wound is fully closed and follow supplied instructions.
