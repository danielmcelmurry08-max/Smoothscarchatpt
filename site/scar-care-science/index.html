01 / Ingredients & delivery

What is a dissolving
micro-tip patch?

BioRNA's manufacturer describes micro-tips made with hyaluronic acid and hydrolyzed RNA. Hyaluronic acid draws in moisture. The micro-tips are designed to dissolve on application; the supplied product instructions define how to apply and wear each patch.

Published scar research also examines micro-tips carrying a specific small interfering RNA, or siRNA, aimed at SPARC, a protein involved in fibrosis. Hydrolyzed RNA and SPARC-targeting siRNA are different descriptions. The research does not, by itself, establish the same mechanism or outcomes for every marketed BioRNA product.

02 / Follow the evidence

From delivery research
to a human clinical trial.

Each source answers a different question. These summaries describe the studied interventions; they are not promises of results from a product purchased here.

2021 · Preclinical delivery study

Protecting and delivering siRNA.

Researchers tested a gelatin hydrogel carrying SPARC-targeting siRNA in cells and a rabbit model of glaucoma surgery. The work explored delivery and postoperative eye scarring. It did not test a consumer skin patch.

Chun et al. Scientific Reports 11:1470.

2022 · Cell & animal study

Testing a dissolving micro-tip platform.

A hyaluronic acid patch carried gelatin–siSPARC nanoplexes. Researchers reported reduced SPARC expression in human dermal fibroblasts and reduced collagen production in a mouse wound model. These preclinical findings do not demonstrate clinical benefit in people.

Chun et al. Biomaterials Science 10:3963–3971.

Read the source ↗
2024 · Dermatology commentary

Why RNA delivery matters.

This article discusses RNA's potential in dermatology, delivery barriers and early scar-care observations. Individual case reports cannot establish how reliably a treatment works or predict another person's outcome.

Chai et al. Annals of the Academy of Medicine, Singapore 53:113–116.

Read the source ↗
Human randomized trial · Online 2024 / issue 2025

siRNA micro-tip patches compared with silicone sheets.

An eight-week trial assigned opposite halves of recent postoperative dermatologic scars to daily SPARC-targeting siRNA patches or silicone sheets. Thirty-two people enrolled; 29 were assessed at day 60.

At day 60, mean scar-volume reductions were 83.8% and 74.1%, respectively: a difference of 9.7 percentage points. These describe change from baseline in both arms, not an 83.8% advantage over silicone. The study reported a significant between-side volume difference.

A small, single-centre study with short follow-up and mostly head-and-neck scars. It does not establish C-section, orthopedic, long-standing keloid or product-wide outcomes. Product equivalence requires confirmation.

Lim et al. British Journal of Dermatology 192:19–26.

03 / C-section scar-care conversations

A thoughtful routine
starts with good questions.

Exploring BioRNA-C after a C-section? Use this checklist with your care team.

  1. Is my incision fully closed and ready for a scar-care product?
  2. Which product, application schedule and coverage are appropriate for my scar?
  3. What evidence applies to this exact formulation and my type of scar?
  4. What changes should I track, and when should we reassess the routine?

Do not apply BioRNA patches to open or infected wounds. Follow the supplied instructions and your clinician's guidance. The trial above did not establish C-section-specific outcomes.

Questions worth asking.

Does “300 micrometres” mean every tip reaches that depth?

No. The clinical trial described 300-micrometre-long microneedles in its study patch. Tip length is not a measurement of penetration depth for every application or commercial product.

Does a one-hour absorption graph prove complete absorption?

A graph needs its measurement method, sample size, tested formulation and endpoint to be interpreted. The supplied one-hour graph alone does not establish a percentage absorbed, systemic absorption or clinical benefit.

Can I expect changes in two or three days?

Individual photographs and early case observations cannot predict your results. The randomized trial evaluated outcomes at 30 and 60 days. Ask what follow-up period is appropriate for your product and scar.
