BioRNA patch vs silicone scar sheets

If you are choosing between a BioRNA patch and a silicone sheet, you are choosing between two different ideas for the same scar. A silicone sheet or tape covers the scar and keeps it hydrated and protected. A BioRNA patch is a dissolving patch: tiny hyaluronic-acid tips melt into the skin overnight and deliver RNA where the scar is forming. This page puts the two side by side — what each one is, how you use it, what it costs, and what the evidence says.

This is a results comparison, not a price one. Silicone costs far less than BioRNA. The case for BioRNA is the result and the mechanism, for the scar that is raised, uncomfortable, or not settling — not for the scar that only needs a simple cover.

Why silicone is the old standard

Silicone is the old standard. It is cheap, most sheets and patches are reusable for days, and waterproof options can be worn while bathing or swimming. For a scar that is settling well and only needs a simple cover, silicone is often enough. What it does not do is deliver anything into the skin — it sits on the surface. That is the gap BioRNA closes: dissolving micro-tips that melt in overnight and deliver RNA where the scar is forming, and in the head-to-head study the BioRNA side came out 30% smaller.

Frida Mom’s silicone patch

Frida Mom makes a silicone C-section patch: 6 reusable patches per pack, each 8 inches long, reusable for up to 7 days each, stored in the included Keep-Clean Case, waterproof. Frida Mom’s own listing says its patches “improve the look and feel of your scar by keeping it hydrated and protected” (fridababy.ca, checked Nov 21, 2026).

What the evidence says

Up to 95% reduction in scar volume.

In a small study, scars treated with the BioRNA patch were down by up to 95% in volume. “Up to” is the highest result, not the average. Individual results vary.

30% lower scar volume than silicone sheets.

In the same split-scar study, scar volume was 30% lower on the BioRNA side than on the silicone-sheet side. This is a direct head-to-head result from that study.

85% saw no worsening after stopping.

Checked 16.1 months after stopping use, 85% had no worsening or elevation of the scar.

These numbers come from a small manufacturer-reported study, not a large trial. A separate published trial in the British Journal of Dermatology (2024) tested a specific siRNA microneedle patch against silicone sheets and found 83.8% vs 74.1% scar-volume reduction. That trial used a different formulation, so its results are not BioRNA product results. There is no published double-blind trial of BioRNA yet; one is in progress. Who each one fits

  • Silicone sheets and tape are for the scar that is settling well and only needs a simple, low-cost cover that hydrates and protects it.
  • BioRNA is for the scar that is raised, uncomfortable, or not settling — and for the person who wants the RNA approach and accepts the price.

If your scar is raised, painful, growing, or infected, see a dermatologist before choosing either one.

Your questions, answered

Questions about BioRNA and silicone

Is BioRNA a silicone patch?

No. BioRNA is a dissolving patch made of hyaluronic-acid micro-tips with hydrolyzed RNA. The tiny tips melt into the skin overnight. A silicone sheet or tape covers the scar and keeps it hydrated, and no ingredient is delivered into the skin.

What did the BioRNA study actually compare?

The split-scar study compared the BioRNA patch directly against silicone sheets: one side of the scar got BioRNA, the other got a silicone sheet. Scar volume was 30% lower on the BioRNA side, and the highest result on the BioRNA side was a 95% reduction in scar volume.

How much does each cost?

BioRNA Daily (Antiscar) is $300 a box; 2 boxes $515, 3 boxes $710, 4 boxes $880. BioRNA-C is $375 a box; 2 boxes $600, 3 boxes $825, 4 boxes $1,000. Silicone sheets cost far less: for example, Frida Mom’s C-section patches are $22.99 a pack at Target, and one pack covers up to 6 weeks.

Which one should I pick for my scar?

It depends on the scar and the budget. Silicone is a simple, low-cost cover that hydrates and protects the scar. BioRNA is for the scar that is raised, uncomfortable, or not settling, and for the person who wants the RNA approach and accepts the price. If your scar is raised, painful, growing, or infected, see a dermatologist before choosing either one.

Can I use either one on a fresh scar?

Only after the wound is fully closed. Do not use a BioRNA patch or a silicone sheet on an open or infected wound. If you are unsure when to start, ask your care team.

Where does the 95% number come from?

From a small manufacturer-reported study, measured with a 3D scanner: scars treated with the BioRNA patch were down by up to 95% in volume. Up to is the highest result, not the average. In the same study, 85% saw no worsening 16.1 months after stopping. Individual results vary.
