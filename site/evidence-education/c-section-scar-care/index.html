C-section scar care

When can I start using a patch on my C-section scar?

Start BioRNA-C only after the entire incision is fully closed. Ask your maternity team to check the incision if you are unsure. Never apply the patch to an open or infected wound.

If you notice increasing redness, pain, or discharge, contact your maternity team promptly. Continue following your discharge instructions for incision care.

How do I use BioRNA-C once my incision is ready?

The BioRNA-C routine uses a fresh patch twice weekly on spaced nights, such as Monday and Friday, for 8–12 hours overnight. Check the supplied instructions and ask your OB-GYN how long to continue.

Your questions, answered

Questions about C-section scar care

When can I start BioRNA-C on my C-section scar?

Only after the entire incision is fully closed. If you are unsure whether your incision is ready, ask your OB-GYN or maternity team to check it first. Never apply the patch to an open or infected wound.

How often do I use BioRNA-C?

Use a fresh patch twice a week, on spaced nights such as Monday and Friday, and wear it for 8–12 hours overnight. Use a new patch for every application — each patch is single use. Ask your OB-GYN how long to continue.

What is BioRNA-C made of?

BioRNA-C is a patch of dissolvable hyaluronic acid micro-tips with hydrolyzed RNA. The micro-tips dissolve into the skin overnight and deliver the RNA where the scar is forming. It is not a silicone sheet or gel.

What if my scar is longer than one patch?

Trim only the adhesive edge, as directed in the supplied instructions, and place patches side by side along the scar. Do not cut through the micro-tip section.

What does the evidence show?

In a small manufacturer-reported study, scars treated with the BioRNA patch were down by up to 95% in volume — up to is the highest reported result, not the average. In the same study, scar volume was 30% lower than with silicone sheets. Individual results vary. See the full evidence, with sources and limits.

What if my incision becomes red, sore, or starts to leak?

Contact your maternity team promptly. Increasing redness, pain, or discharge can be signs of infection, and your care team should check the incision before you use any scar-care product on it.
